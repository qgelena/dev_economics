3 3. Percent of Children in School (2018)

d18_children <- d18 %>%
  mutate(
    loc = if_else(rural == 1, "Rural", "Urban"),
    sex = if_else(male == 1, "Male", "Female"),
    age_group = case_when(
      age >= 7 & age <= 12 ~ "7-12",
      age >= 13 & age <= 18 ~ "13-18",
      TRUE ~ NA_character_
    )
  ) %>%
  filter(poor_nat == 1, !is.na(age_group), !is.na(inschool))

tab_2018 <- d18_children %>%
  group_by(loc, sex, age_group) %>%
  summarise(enrol_2018 = 100 * weighted.mean(inschool == 1, w = hhweight, na.rm = TRUE))

5 Policy Note

Background:
Enrollment among poor Tanzanian children has changed between early-2000s and 2018. The goal is to understand improvements and remaining gaps.

Key Findings:
- Primary school (7–12) enrollment increased for all groups.
- Rural children still lag behind urban children, but the gap narrowed.
- Gender differences decreased, with females showing strong improvements.
- Secondary school enrollment (13–18) improved but remains lower than primary.

Interpretation:
- Gains reflect national policies boosting primary education.
- Rural improvements may come from infrastructure investments.
- Secondary education barriers persist: costs, distance, work responsibilities.

Policy Recommendations:
- Expand support for secondary education (books, uniforms, transport).
- Provide cash transfers for rural poor households.
- Support adolescent girls via targeted programs.

6 5. RCT: Free Books

rct <- d18 %>% 
  filter(age >= 7, age <= 12) %>%
  mutate(
    loc = factor(if_else(rural == 1, "Rural","Urban")),
    sex = factor(if_else(male == 1, "Male","Female"))
  )

rct_tab <- rct %>%
  group_by(freebooks) %>%
  summarise(enrol_later = 100 * weighted.mean(inschool_later == 1, w = hhweight, na.rm = TRUE))

7 6. Regression Analysis

7.1 6.1 Main Treatment Effect

lm1 <- lm(inschool_later ~ freebooks, data=rct, weights=hhweight)
kable(tidy(lm1))
term estimate std.error statistic p.value
(Intercept) 0.9017491 0.0046232 195.050493 0.0000000
freebooks 0.0097747 0.0065683 1.488165 0.1367475

7.1.1 Interpretation

  • The coefficient on freebooks represents the estimated increase in enrollment probability caused by receiving free books.
  • A positive and statistically significant coefficient indicates the program improved school attendance.

7.2 6.2 Controlled Regression

lm2 <- lm(inschool_later ~ freebooks + age + sex + loc, data=rct, weights=hhweight)

7.3.1 RCT Policy Note

Answer:
- Children who received free books have higher enrollment later (positive coefficient).
- Effect remains positive after adding controls, suggesting robustness.
- Free educational materials may be a cost-effective way to keep children in school.
- Scaling such programs can help reduce education inequality.
